## Validation Notes

### Test 1 — Onliness Test

> *"[Product] is the only AI proposal builder that interviews homeowners in plain language and outputs a maker-quotable brief in trade language — purpose-built for custom kitchen and cabinet studios."*

- **Pass.** "Only" is defensible against the mapped alternatives:
  - AI receptionists (LeadTruffle, Avoca, Smith.ai) speak plain → produce *contact summaries*, not trade briefs
  - AI render tools (RoomGPT, Spacely) produce *images*, not briefs
  - CAD (Cyncly, 2020 DesignFLEX) produces *files*, not interviews
  - Forms (Typeform, Tally) are linear and abandoned past 7 fields
  - Houzz Pro intake is static and notoriously low-quality
  - Skipp/Palazzo/DecorX are B2C and route to specific suppliers

The "purpose-built for custom kitchen and cabinet studios" qualifier makes the dual-tone differentiator real. **The basic onliness statement does not feel like a stretch.**

### Test 2 — Moore Statement

- **Pass.** Every field is fillable with confidence:
  - Target: defined by characteristics, not demographics
  - Need: dollarizable (hours/week + design-fee revenue trade-off)
  - Category: searchable, claimable subcategory
  - Benefit: anchored in customer language ("ghosted," "lead quality," "stress level")
  - Alternative: named, current, painful (Houzz Pro intake + paid design fee)
  - Differentiation: dual-tone output, integrations, white-label

### Test 3 — Ries/Trout Mental Ladder

- **Can you state it in 10 words or fewer?** *"AI proposal builder for custom kitchen makers."* (7 words.) ✅
- **Does it claim ONE clear rung?** Yes — *"AI intake that produces a maker-quotable brief."* ✅
- **Is the rung available?** Yes — confirmed by category research; nobody owns this exact niche today. ✅
- **Would a customer use these words to describe you?** Likely. Test phrase: *"It's like Houzz Pro's intake form, but it actually understands kitchens — and it doesn't sell my lead three times."* ✅

### Overall Positioning Strength: **Moderate-Strong**

Three caveats that prevent a clean "Strong" rating:
1. Three of the unique attributes (multimodal handling, conversation-first UX, integration round-trip) are execution-dependent — must be real at launch.
2. "Proposal builder" carries some PandaDoc-misread risk; needs careful copy.
3. The 12–18-month execution window before Cyncly/Houzz Pro ship competing intake means the positioning is correct but time-bound. Speed matters.

## Red Flags

- **Onliness claim depends on unfalsifiable competitors.** The "only" claim partly hinges on what Skipp / Palazzo / DecorX actually do — but their B2B positioning isn't publicly disclosed [Data gap]. If any of them already does dual-tone interview + maker-quotable brief, the Onliness statement weakens. Founder-led validation calls (or product comparison demos) recommended before publicly claiming "only."
- **Three execution-dependent attributes** (multimodal input, conversation-first UX, integration round-trip) must be real at launch or the Moore statement collapses into roadmap-positioning, which the honesty protocol explicitly rejects.
- **PandaDoc / Proposify misread** of "proposal builder" is a real risk — could pull buyer's mental model away from the AI-interview wedge toward generic doc-template tools.

## Yellow Flags

- **Tagline "Stop charging for proposals"** could be misread by some makers as "we make proposals free for clients" — i.e. devaluing the craft. Always pair with the second-half framing: *"…and stop chasing tire-kickers anyway."*
- **"AI proposal builder for custom kitchen makers"** is 7 words but combines two qualifiers ("AI" + "for custom kitchen makers") — risks sounding niche-y to investors who want big-market narratives. Investor framing should reach for the parent category ("AI sales enablement for trades") in the first sentence and narrow afterwards.
- **The 30-second elevator pitch is dense.** Test for time and trim if needed; consider a 15-second variant for very informal contexts.
- **No real customer quotes available yet at launch.** All taglines and one-liners are constructed from positioning theory + industry verbatims, not customer testimonials. Expect first 5–10 design partners to inform meaningful refinement.

## Data Gaps

- **No A/B test results** on which tagline variant performs best — recommend testing 3–4 in cold outbound and landing pages within 90 days of launch.
- **No customer-language validation** beyond the verbatims captured in research; specifically, no testing of whether makers actually say "AI proposal builder" or default to "AI receptionist" / "AI intake" / "AI lead form" when describing the category.
- **No conversion-rate baseline** for any of the channel-specific one-liners; track first 100 cold-email opens and demo bookings to inform iteration.
- **Investor-pitch positioning** is structured but untested with actual VCs in the space (PropTech Farm, Pace Ventures, etc.). Iterate after the first 5 investor conversations.
